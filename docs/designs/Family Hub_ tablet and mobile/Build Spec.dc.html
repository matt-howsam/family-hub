Two hard rules

An icon may never carry status — it takes --fh-ink-body or a person hue, never amber. And it never appears without its word. A pictogram that must be interpreted is slower than a word for four people of different ages, and unlike a word it can be got wrong.

The teal split is the one rule most likely to be broken. --fh-teal measures 4.6:1 on white — over the line by a hair — but 4.4:1 on the sunken card and 4.0:1 on the ready tint — it fails on both, and those are the two grounds it most often lands on. So it is legal at 24px+ (where 3:1 suffices) or as a fill, and nowhere else. --fh-teal-ink clears every ground at every size. Amber has no such split, which is why it can carry a 10px label. Worth a lint rule.

Each person is a tint pair plus a deep initial of the same hue — so a disc reads as a person, never as a status. Every ratio above is measured against the gradient's light stop, not the card behind it, which is where a tinted system usually fails silently. That rule caught the water ramp: at 145deg the lightest stop lands bottom-right, exactly where the hero's stats row sits, and the original light stop gave white only 2.9:1. The ramp is now capped so white clears 4.5:1 against every stop — the light stop's 4.7:1 is the governing figure, not the dark stop's 6.3:1. The kid slab needs its own deeper ink (--fh-tom-slab-ink, --fh-rose-slab-ink) because it carries a 34px word and an 11px label on the same fill.

The metre rule
20px
min-glance

The floor for anything a passer-by must read without stopping. Row titles, answers, money, next actions, day counts. ~4.3mm cap-height at 810px across a 174mm panel.

16px
min-legible

The floor for text you may lean in for — a second-line qualifier, a note. Readable at arm's length, not across the room.

10px
min-meta · labels only

Never information. If a fact is set at 10px it is not on the wall — it is on the phone. This is the line that stops the display becoming an admin panel.

The rule, in priority order
1
Ambient light under 12 lux for 60 seconds

Via AmbientLightSensor where available. A kitchen at 10pm with just the rangehood light measures ~8–15 lux, so the threshold sits above a dark room and below someone having turned a light on. 60 seconds stops a passing cloud or an opened fridge door from flipping it.

2
No sensor → 20:30 to 06:00 local

The fallback, and probably what ships first. 20:30 is after the kids are down and before the adults are.

3
Anyone approaches → full daylight for 90 seconds

Motion within ~2.5m or any touch. Someone walking up gets the bright screen before they arrive — a wall you have to wake is a wall that has stopped working. Then it re-evaluates.

4
Never between 06:00 and 20:30, whatever the sensor says

A dim screen on an overcast Tuesday morning is a broken screen. The daytime hard-override is the guard against a well-meaning sensor.

It is not a colour swap

The content shortens. Kid slabs, chore cards and what's-on stop printing. The wall holds time, date, tomorrow's week letter, the house line, and any single genuinely-overdue row. Nothing anyone can act on at 10pm earns space.

Brightness drops to 35% in the kiosk shell, not via opacity. Faded type on a bright layout still emits bright light — the panel is the lightbox, not the pixels.

The teal split reverses. By day --fh-teal is 24px-and-up only; at night #4FBFC4 is safe at every size and the dark teal is unusable. Both map to the same two tokens, so components need no night-specific code.

Shadows are replaced by surface value. A drop shadow does nothing on a dark ground; cards separate by being one step lighter than the page.

Never on the phone. A phone is read at 30cm, has its own auto-brightness, and should inherit OS dark mode. Night state is a property of a wall, not of the product.
