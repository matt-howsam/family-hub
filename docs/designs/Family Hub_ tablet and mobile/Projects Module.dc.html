Waiting is now permanently calm

Your rule, and it is better than mine. The moment the expected date passes it becomes your move, so Paint the house has lifted into Needs you and Waiting holds one genuinely calm row with no amber anywhere in it. The group can now be trusted at a glance, which is the only thing that makes a "nothing to do" group worth having. Two consequences worth noting: Needs you is three rows at month three rather than two, and the chase keeps its own words — "Chase Brett — the quote was due 11 days ago" — so the row still reads as a chase, not a fresh start.

Day one sorts smallest-first, and absence has gone quiet

With no history the order was arbitrary, so it now sorts by estimate ascending: under stair storage at $2,000 leads, the kitchen at $40,000+ closes. Unpriced jobs sit between them — cheapest first, unpriced next, biggest last — which keeps the two real numbers at the ends of the list where they carry. And "no estimate" is gone: an unpriced row simply shows its stage. Four repetitions of the word no in the largest right-hand slot was the loudest thing on the launch screen, which is precisely backwards. From day 22 the sort reverts to days-descending.

Day one still has no day counts

Unchanged, because it is the thing that makes the screen work. On 8 September nothing has ever moved, so there is no honest number to print — "0 days" is meaningless and a backdated count would be invented. Removing the counter removes the indictment automatically, and the framing line dates the start of the record: "nothing has moved yet, because nothing has been in here to move — the clock starts today."

One thing the cuts leave exposed

Dropping the fridge detail is right, and it means the list row is now the only thing the wall ever says about a job. That raises the stakes on the next-action line considerably — it is no longer a summary with a fuller version behind it, it is the entire fridge-side interface. It survives that here because every Needs-you row carries its next action in full, but it does mean a row with a vague next action becomes a dead end rather than an invitation to tap through. Worth a rule when the jobs go in: if the next action doesn't name a verb and a person or thing, it isn't finished.
