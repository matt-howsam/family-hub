2d What this direction wins and loses

Both are real options and I'd build either. But two of your own earlier notes argue against this one, and it would be dishonest of me to hand it over without saying so.

What it wins

Lighter, friendlier, much less severe — the family register problem largely dissolves, because the whole product now sits in the warmer voice and the household rows are distinguished by a single amber wash rather than a different design language. Rose is thirteen and this is unambiguously the version she'd rather have on the fridge. The soft gradients also give the water block somewhere to be genuinely beautiful.

The lightbox problem — your own note

You wrote that a white screen at full brightness, permanently on in a kitchen, is a lightbox at 9pm — and you were right. This is that screen. The cream ground was doing real work: it is roughly 8% darker than white at the same backlight, which is the difference between a warm object and a lamp. If we go this way, the night state stops being optional and has to be designed alongside it — probably a genuinely dark variant after 8pm rather than the "shorten it" approach.

It loses the connection to the paper

The register and the scorecard are typeset documents with a serif display face and a warm accent, and the brief's position was that this instinct is correct and worth carrying across. This version doesn't carry it — it is a well-made app, where the other is a well-made household document that happens to be on a screen. That may be exactly the trade you want. It is worth making knowingly rather than as a side effect of preferring the type.

The hybrid, if you want one

Keep this sans, this radius and these soft gradients, and put them on a very light warm ground rather than white — around #FBF9F5, which is far closer to this direction than to the cream but still reads as a surface rather than a light source. Money and the week letter stay serif; everything else goes sans. That keeps the paper's one strongest signal, kills the severity, and solves the 9pm problem. Say the word and I'll build it as 3a.
