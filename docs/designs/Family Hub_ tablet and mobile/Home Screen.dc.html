The saturated hues are darker than "bright" usually means, and that is deliberate. Every one of these grounds carries knocked-out type, and a genuinely bright fill drops 14px micro-caps below 4.5:1 — so each person's hue is pushed to the most saturated value that still clears it on the label as well as the headline. Matt 4.9:1, Renée 5.0:1, Rose 6.1:1, Tom 4.7:1, water 4.8:1. They read as colour from across the kitchen and stay readable up close.

What changed, and the two things I'd still check

All six notes are in. Two of them I want to flag rather than quietly declare solved.

The two R's — not solved, only improved

Renée has moved to terracotta and Rose to violet, which is the furthest apart I can push them while every disc still carries knocked-out type at 4.5:1. At a metre they are now clearly different circles. But you are right that this wants confirming rather than assuming, and the honest position is that two adjacent discs sharing a letter is a weak identifier no matter what colour they are. The real fix is the Open Peeps faces — a face is recognised pre-linguistically and neither the letter nor the hue has to carry the load. Worth testing on the actual iPad at the actual distance before the illustration lands.

Chore rates — visible, and still swappable

1b shows per-job rates on the paid work (mow $15, bathrooms $20, windows $15) and no rate on the contribution chores (bins, dishwasher, vacuum), with an earned figure rather than an owed one. That is the per-chore model rendered — but the row shape is unchanged if you go flat-weekly: the rate simply drops out of the chip and the figure becomes the week's flat amount. Nothing about the layout assumes which model you pick, which was the point of leaving it open.

Still mine, still plausible, not real

The 142 days on the kitchen, the chore rates, the swell and tide figures, and the four what's-on events. Everything else is from the artefacts. The what's-on row is built to tolerate an empty week — with nothing in it the block simply doesn't print, exactly like a module row, and the rows above it grow into the space.
